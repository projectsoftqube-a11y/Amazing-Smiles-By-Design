## When Should a Child First See a Dentist?

Your child's first regular dental visit should take place just after their first birthday. Early visits let us monitor development, prevent problems and help your child get used to the dental office.

### What happens at the first visit

The first appointment is usually short and involves very little treatment:

- You may sit in the dental chair and hold your child during the exam
- The dentist gently examines your child's teeth and gums
- Digital X-rays may be taken if needed to check for decay or monitor permanent teeth
- Teeth may be cleaned and topical fluoride applied to help protect against decay
- We review how to clean and care for your child's teeth at home

Most importantly, we begin building a trusting relationship between your child and their dentist.

## Preparing Your Child for the Dentist

Prepare your child the way you would for a first haircut, calmly and positively:

- Bring your child for a preview of the office
- Read children's books about visiting the dentist
- Explain simply what the dentist will do
- Speak positively about your own dental visits

## Children's Dental Exams, Cleanings, Fluoride and Sealants

At each visit, the dentist will:

- Examine the mouth, teeth and gums
- Evaluate habits such as thumb sucking
- Check fluoride exposure and dietary risks
- Teach proper brushing and cleaning techniques
- Recommend a schedule for regular checkups

Tooth decay doesn't have to be a normal part of childhood. We use [dental sealants](/general-dentistry/dental-sealants/), protective coatings bonded to the chewing surfaces of cavity-prone back teeth, to help block the bacteria and food particles that cause cavities.

## Why Children Get Cavities

Cavities are most often caused by frequent sugar exposure combined with inconsistent brushing. Each time a child eats, bacteria in the mouth digest sugars and create acids that last about 20 minutes, while the enamel is vulnerable. Cavity risk depends on how often your child snacks, a sugary or carbohydrate-rich diet, oral hygiene habits and saliva.

### Cavity prevention tips at home

- Limit frequent meals and snacks
- Encourage brushing, flossing and rinsing daily
- Monitor sugary drinks and juices
- Avoid sticky foods that cling to teeth
- Make treats part of meals instead of separate snacks
- Choose nutritious snacks whenever possible

## Baby Teeth and Dental Development

The first baby teeth usually appear between 6 and 8 months, starting with the two lower front teeth. By around 2½ years old, most children have all 20 primary teeth. Between ages 5 and 6, permanent teeth begin to come in. Every child develops a little differently.

Baby teeth matter: they hold space for permanent teeth, support chewing and nutrition, help with speech and contribute to your child's appearance and confidence.
