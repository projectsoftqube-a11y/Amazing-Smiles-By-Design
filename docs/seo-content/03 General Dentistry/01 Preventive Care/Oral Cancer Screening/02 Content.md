## Why Oral Cancer Screenings Matter

The inside of the mouth is lined with a tissue called oral mucosa, which usually looks smooth and coral pink. Changes in this tissue can signal abnormal growth or early oral cancer. Dentists are trained to spot subtle changes that most people wouldn't notice. Early oral cancer often isn't painful, which is why professional screenings matter.

### Warning signs we look for

- Reddish patches (erythroplasia)
- White patches (leukoplakia)
- A sore that doesn't heal or bleeds easily
- Thickened tissue or lumps in the mouth
- Persistent throat irritation or hoarseness
- Difficulty chewing or swallowing

These changes can appear on the lips, cheeks, gums, tongue, palate or throat, and sometimes on the face or neck.

## What Happens During an Oral Cancer Screening

The screening takes just a few minutes during your checkup and needs no special preparation. Many patients don't even notice it, because it's part of the dentist's normal exam:

- **Tongue:** the dentist gently holds and extends your tongue with gauze to check the sides and underside.
- **Palate:** the roof of your mouth is checked for patches, lumps or soft areas.
- **Lips and cheeks:** gently felt for bumps or thickened areas.
- **Neck and jaw:** checked for enlarged lymph nodes.
- **Tonsils and throat:** viewed for redness, swelling, asymmetry or growths.
- **Floor of the mouth:** gently pressed from inside and below the jaw to feel for lumps.

## Symptoms You Should Have Checked

These symptoms don't automatically mean cancer, but have a dentist evaluate them if they persist:

- A sore in the mouth that doesn't heal
- Red or white patches inside the mouth
- Tenderness or numbness in the lips or tongue
- Difficulty chewing or swallowing, or trouble moving your tongue
- Persistent mouth pain
- Speech changes
- Rough or crusted areas inside the mouth
- A lump in the neck
- Chronic sore throat or hoarseness, or a feeling that something is stuck in your throat

## Reducing Your Risk

Anyone can develop oral cancer, but smoking or using tobacco and drinking excessive alcohol increase the risk. Human papillomavirus (HPV) is associated with some cancers at the back of the mouth, including the tonsils and base of the tongue, even in people who don't smoke. Seeing your dentist every six months lets these areas be checked regularly.

## Oral Cancer Screening FAQs

### What is an oral cancer screening?
An oral cancer screening is a quick, painless exam in which the dentist checks your lips, tongue, cheeks, palate, throat, floor of the mouth and neck for unusual patches, lumps or sores that could be early signs of oral cancer.

### Does an oral cancer screening hurt?
No. The screening is painless, takes just a few minutes and needs no special preparation.

### How often should I have an oral cancer screening?
At Amazing Smiles By Design, an oral cancer screening is part of your routine dental exam, and most patients see the dentist every six months.

### What are the early signs of oral cancer?
Early signs can include red or white patches, a sore that doesn't heal, lumps or thickened tissue, persistent hoarseness and difficulty chewing or swallowing. Early oral cancer is often painless.
