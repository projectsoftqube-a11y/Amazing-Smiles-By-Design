## What Are Dental Sealants?

The chewing surfaces of the back teeth (molars and premolars) have deep grooves where food and bacteria collect. These grooves can be hard to clean with a toothbrush, which makes them a common place for cavities to start. A sealant is a thin, tooth-colored or clear coating that fills and seals these grooves so plaque has fewer places to hide.

---

## Sealants for Kids: Who Should Get Them?

Sealants are most often recommended for children, because the back teeth are prone to cavities as they come in and kids are still learning to brush well. The dentist will tell you whether sealants are a good choice for your child at their checkup.

---

## How Sealants Are Applied

Applying sealants is usually quick and doesn't involve drilling:

1. The tooth surface is cleaned and dried.
2. A solution is applied to help the sealant bond to the enamel.
3. The sealant is painted into the grooves of the tooth.
4. The sealant is hardened, usually with a special curing light.

The dentist checks your child's sealants at regular visits.

---

## Sealants Work Best With Good Habits at Home

Sealants protect the chewing surfaces, but brushing, flossing and a healthy diet still matter. Limit frequent snacks and sugary drinks, avoid sticky foods that cling to teeth, and make treats part of meals instead of separate snacks.
