# Oral Hygiene: Daily Care Between Visits

Good oral hygiene is one of the most important things you can do to protect your teeth, gums and overall health. Your mouth constantly produces bacteria that form a sticky film called plaque. Without daily care, plaque builds up along the gum line and between teeth and hardens into tartar, which can only be removed at a dental visit. ## Why Oral Hygiene Matters

Many dental problems start with plaque. Once it hardens into tartar (calculus), it irritates the gums and can lead to gum disease. Poor oral hygiene can cause:

- Gum inflammation and bleeding
- Periodontal infections
- Bone loss around teeth
- Tooth decay and cavities
- Persistent bad breath
- Tooth sensitivity
- Loose teeth or tooth loss

## How to Brush Your Teeth Properly

Brush at least twice a day with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste:

1. Angle the brush at about 45 degrees where your gums meet your teeth.
2. Use small, gentle circular motions on the outer surfaces.
3. Use light pressure so the bristles reach slightly between teeth and under the gum line.
4. Brush the inside surfaces the same way.
5. Hold the brush upright to clean the inside of your front teeth.
6. Use short strokes on the chewing surfaces of your back teeth.
7. Lightly brush your gums.
8. Rinse well to remove loosened plaque.

Brushing in front of a mirror helps you avoid missed spots. If brushing causes discomfort or bleeding that doesn't improve, ask our team for advice on your technique.

## Flossing: Cleaning Where Your Toothbrush Can't Reach

The tight spaces between teeth are where gum disease often starts, because toothbrush bristles can't reach them. Floss daily:

1. Use about 18 inches of floss so you have a clean section for each tooth.
2. Wrap it around your middle fingers and guide it with your thumbs and forefingers.
3. Slide it gently between your teeth with a back-and-forth motion.
4. Curve it into a C-shape against each tooth.
5. Move it up and down along the tooth and just under the gum line.
6. Clean both sides of every gap.

Mild bleeding when you first start flossing regularly is usually a sign of gum inflammation from plaque. As you keep flossing, your gums generally become healthier and bleed less.

## Choosing Oral Hygiene Products

- **Electric toothbrushes:** consistent brushing motion that is very effective at removing plaque.
- **Fluoride toothpaste:** strengthens enamel and helps reduce tooth decay.
- **Anti-plaque mouth rinses:** rinses approved by the American Dental Association can help control the bacteria that cause gum disease.
- **Interproximal brushes:** small brushes for cleaning between teeth where floss is difficult.
- **Water flossers (oral irrigators):** rinse away food and bacteria, used alongside brushing and flossing, not instead of them.

Our team can help you choose the right products for your needs.

## Caring for Sensitive Teeth

Sensitivity to hot or cold, or after some dental treatments, is often temporary and improves with good oral hygiene. If it continues, the dentist may recommend toothpaste for sensitive teeth, a special mouth rinse, changes to your brushing habits or products, or professional fluoride treatments.

## Oral Hygiene FAQs

### How often should I brush my teeth?
Brush at least twice a day with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste, using small, gentle circular motions at a 45-degree angle to the gum line.

### Is it normal for gums to bleed when I start flossing?
Mild bleeding when you first start flossing regularly is usually a sign of gum inflammation caused by plaque. It typically decreases as you floss consistently. If bleeding doesn't improve, ask the dentist.

### What is the best toothbrush to use?
A soft-bristled toothbrush works well for most people, and electric toothbrushes can be very effective at removing plaque because they provide a consistent brushing motion. Our team can recommend products for your needs.

### Can a water flosser replace regular flossing?
A water flosser can help rinse away food and bacteria, but it should be used alongside brushing and flossing rather than replacing them.
