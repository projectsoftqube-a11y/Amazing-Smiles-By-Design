## What Happens at a Dental Exam and Cleaning

### Your dental exam

During your exam, the dentist will:

- Review your medical and dental history
- Discuss any concerns or changes you've noticed
- Examine your teeth for cavities, cracks or damage
- Check existing dental work such as fillings or crowns
- Evaluate your bite and jaw function
- Assess the health of your gums and supporting bone

Many patients also use this time to ask about cosmetic or restorative treatments such as teeth whitening, dental implants or orthodontics.

### Gum health check

The dentist or hygienist measures the small spaces between your teeth and gums, called periodontal pockets. Healthy gums usually have shallow pockets. Deeper pockets can be a sign of gum disease, so measuring them helps us catch it early.

### Oral cancer screening

Every exam includes an [oral cancer screening](/general-dentistry/oral-cancer-screening/): a careful look at your lips, tongue, cheeks, throat and surrounding tissues for anything unusual.

### Professional teeth cleaning

Even with excellent brushing and flossing, plaque can harden into tartar (calculus) that can't be removed at home. During your cleaning, our hygienist removes plaque and tartar, especially along the gum line and between teeth, then polishes your teeth with a prophy paste that smooths the enamel and helps resist new buildup. We'll also show you where plaque tends to collect so you can improve your routine at home.

## Digital Dental X-Rays

Some problems develop where they can't be seen during a visual exam. Digital X-rays help detect:

- Cavities between teeth
- Bone loss related to gum disease
- Infections or abscesses
- Impacted teeth
- Jawbone abnormalities
- Hidden damage

Digital X-rays typically need to be taken once a year, although this varies with your oral health needs. Modern digital imaging produces highly detailed images while using significantly less radiation than traditional X-ray systems.

## How Often You Need a Checkup

Most patients benefit from a dental exam and cleaning every six months. Patients who have been treated for gum disease often need [periodontal maintenance](/general-dentistry/periodontal-maintenance/) every three to four months instead.

Skipping checkups lets small problems grow unnoticed. Regular exams help detect tooth decay and gum disease early, monitor bone health, detect oral cancer early and prevent more extensive treatment.
