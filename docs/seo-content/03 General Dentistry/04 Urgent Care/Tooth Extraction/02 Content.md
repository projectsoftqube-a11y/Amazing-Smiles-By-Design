## Why a Tooth May Need to Be Removed

Extraction is usually needed when a tooth is damaged beyond what a filling, crown or other treatment can repair. Common reasons include:

- Severe decay that has destroyed a large part of the tooth
- Advanced gum disease that has weakened the supporting bone
- A broken or fractured tooth that can't be restored
- Impacted teeth that can't fully come in through the gums
- Teeth that are poorly positioned in the mouth
- Creating space before orthodontic treatment

### Are there alternatives?

Depending on the tooth, alternatives may include a [crown](/restorative-dentistry/dental-crowns/), [root canal therapy](/restorative-dentistry/non-surgical-root-canal/) or periodontal treatment. If a tooth can't be restored safely, extraction may be the best way to prevent infection or damage to neighboring teeth.

## Impacted Teeth, Including Wisdom Teeth

Impacted teeth, which can't fully come in through the gums, are one of the common reasons for extraction. Wisdom teeth are often the teeth affected. The dentist will evaluate your teeth, often with X-rays, and explain your options. If you need care from another dental professional, we refer you to carefully vetted colleagues.

## What to Expect During a Tooth Extraction

1. **Numbing.** The dentist numbs the tooth, the gum and the nearby bone with a local anesthetic.
2. **Removal.** Once the area is fully numb, the tooth is gently loosened and removed. You may feel firm pressure as the tooth is rocked back and forth, but you shouldn't feel pain, because the nerves that carry pain are blocked while the nerves that sense pressure are still active. If you feel discomfort, tell the dentist right away.
3. **Sectioning (sometimes).** If a tooth is firmly anchored or has curved roots, the dentist may divide it into smaller sections and remove each piece. This is a common, safe technique that reduces stress on the surrounding bone.

## Tooth Extraction Recovery

### The first hour
Bite down on a gauze pad for 30 to 45 minutes to help a blood clot form in the socket. If minor bleeding continues, replace the gauze and apply gentle pressure.

### Avoiding dry socket
The blood clot protects the bone and nerves while the socket heals. Losing it too early can cause a painful condition called dry socket. For the first 72 hours, avoid:

- Rinsing your mouth vigorously
- Drinking through a straw
- Smoking
- Drinking alcohol
- Brushing right next to the extraction site

### Pain and swelling
Mild discomfort and swelling are normal and usually improve within a couple of days. A cold compress on the outside of your face during the first 24 hours can help, and swelling typically starts to improve within 48 hours. Use the pain medication your dentist recommends or prescribes. If antibiotics are prescribed, take the full course exactly as directed.

### What to eat after a tooth extraction
On the day of your extraction, eat soft foods and drink plenty of fluids. Return to your normal diet gradually as you feel more comfortable.

### Getting back to normal
Rest on the first day and avoid strenuous activity, which can increase bleeding. After about 24 hours, resume brushing and flossing, taking care around the extraction site. Most patients feel ready for their regular routine within a few days.

### When to call us
Call (215) 639-5331 if you have heavy or persistent bleeding, severe pain that doesn't improve, swelling that lasts more than two to three days, a reaction to medication, or signs of infection such as fever or unusual discharge.

## Tooth Extraction FAQs

### Does a tooth extraction hurt?
The tooth, gum and nearby bone are numbed with a local anesthetic, so the extraction should not be painful. You may feel firm pressure, because the nerves that sense pressure are still active. Tell the dentist right away if you feel discomfort.

### How long does it take to recover from a tooth extraction?
Swelling typically starts to improve within 48 hours, and most patients feel comfortable returning to their regular routine within a few days.

### How do I avoid dry socket?
Protect the blood clot. For the first 72 hours, avoid vigorous rinsing, drinking through a straw, smoking, alcohol and brushing right next to the extraction site.

### What can I eat after a tooth extraction?
Eat soft foods and drink plenty of fluids on the day of the procedure, then gradually return to your normal diet as you feel more comfortable.
