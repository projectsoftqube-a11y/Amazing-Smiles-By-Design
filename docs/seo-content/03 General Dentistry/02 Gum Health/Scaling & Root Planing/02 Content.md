## Signs of Gum Disease

Gum disease often develops quietly. You may need a deep cleaning if you notice:

- Bleeding gums when brushing or flossing
- Red, swollen or tender gums
- Persistent bad breath that doesn't improve with brushing
- Gum recession, or teeth that look longer than before
- Loose teeth, in more advanced cases

During an exam, the dentist or hygienist measures the spaces between your teeth and gums with a periodontal probe. Healthy gums usually measure between one and three millimeters. At your exam, the dentist or hygienist may also find tartar buildup beneath the gum line. Deeper pockets trap bacteria that can damage the gums and the bone that supports your teeth.

## What Scaling and Root Planing Involves

### Scaling
Plaque and hardened tartar are removed from above and below the gum line with special instruments, including areas along the roots that brushing and flossing can't reach.

### Root planing
The roots of the teeth are gently smoothed. Rough root surfaces let bacteria cling more easily, so smoothing them helps:

- Remove bacterial toxins
- Reduce gum inflammation
- Allow the gums to reattach more securely
- Make it harder for bacteria to build up again

---

## What to Expect During Treatment

Treatment is usually done in sections of the mouth and may take one or more visits, depending on how much gum disease is present. The area may be numbed with local anesthesia so the cleaning can be done comfortably and thoroughly.

1. Plaque and tartar are removed from above and below the gum line.
2. The tooth roots are smoothed.
3. Deep periodontal pockets are cleaned to remove bacteria and infected tissue.
4. In some cases, antibacterial solutions or a localized antibiotic such as [Arestin](/general-dentistry/arestin/) may be applied.

---

## Deep Cleaning vs. Regular Cleaning

A routine cleaning focuses mainly on the visible surfaces of the teeth. Scaling and root planing treats the areas below the gum line where periodontal disease develops. It is usually recommended for moderate gum disease (periodontitis), when a regular cleaning is no longer enough to remove the infection.

---

## Recovery and Aftercare

Mild tenderness, gum sensitivity or slight swelling for a few days is normal while the gums heal. Many patients soon notice less swelling, less bleeding when brushing and fresher breath. To keep those improvements:

- Brush gently twice a day with a soft-bristled toothbrush
- Floss daily
- Use an antimicrobial mouth rinse if recommended
- Avoid very hard or crunchy foods for a short time
- Keep your periodontal maintenance visits

After treatment, many patients move to [periodontal maintenance](/general-dentistry/periodontal-maintenance/) visits every three to four months to keep bacteria from building up again.

## Deep Cleaning FAQs

### Is a deep cleaning necessary?
A deep cleaning is usually recommended when the dentist finds signs of moderate gum disease (periodontitis), such as pockets of about 4 millimeters or more along with inflammation or bone loss, rather than the healthy one to three millimeters. Removing bacteria below the gum line helps stop gum disease from progressing and protects the bone that supports your teeth.

### Does scaling and root planing hurt?
The area being treated may be numbed with local anesthesia so the cleaning can be done comfortably. Mild tenderness or sensitivity for a few days afterward is normal.

### How many visits does a deep cleaning take?
Scaling and root planing is usually done in sections of the mouth and may take one or more visits, depending on how much gum disease is present.

### What's the difference between a deep cleaning and a regular cleaning?
A regular cleaning focuses mainly on the visible surfaces of the teeth. A deep cleaning removes plaque, tartar and bacteria from below the gum line and smooths the tooth roots so the gums can heal.

### Do I need a periodontist for gum disease treatment?
Scaling and root planing is a non-surgical treatment provided at Amazing Smiles By Design in Bensalem. If you need care from a periodontist or another dental professional, we refer you to carefully vetted colleagues.
