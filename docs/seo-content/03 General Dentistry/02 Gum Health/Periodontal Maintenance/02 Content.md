## Why Maintenance Matters After a Deep Cleaning

Gum disease (periodontal disease) is an infection of the gums caused by bacteria in plaque. When plaque isn't removed, it hardens into tartar that can only be removed professionally. Bacteria release toxins that irritate the gums, which can pull away from the teeth and form pockets where more bacteria collect. Without treatment, gum disease can damage the bone and tissue that hold your teeth in place.

After gum disease is treated with [scaling and root planing](/general-dentistry/scaling-and-root-planing/), ongoing care is essential to keep it from coming back. At each periodontal maintenance visit, our team carefully removes plaque and bacteria from around the gum pockets and monitors the health of your gums and bone.

## How Often You Need Periodontal Maintenance

Many patients benefit from periodontal maintenance every three to four months, although your schedule may vary with your individual needs. Regular maintenance helps stabilize gum health and stop the infection from progressing again.

## Signs of Gum Disease to Watch For

The earliest stage, gingivitis, is often reversible with professional care and better oral hygiene. Warning signs include:

- Red or swollen gums
- Bleeding while brushing or flossing
- Tender or sensitive gums
- Persistent bad breath
- Gums pulling away from the teeth

### Risk factors

Plaque is the main cause, but your risk also rises with smoking or tobacco use, diabetes, chronic stress, teeth grinding or clenching, certain medications, poor nutrition and genetics. At your exams, our team considers these factors when planning your care.

### Gum health and overall health

Advanced periodontal disease has been associated with conditions including diabetes, heart disease, respiratory illness, osteoporosis and complications during pregnancy. Keeping your gums healthy is part of supporting your overall wellness.

## Treatment Options for Gum Disease

- **Scaling and root planing (deep cleaning):** removes plaque and tartar below the gum line and smooths the roots so the gums can heal. Medicated mouth rinses or antibiotics such as [Arestin](/general-dentistry/arestin/) may also be recommended.
- **Advanced treatments:** if gum disease has caused tissue or bone loss, procedures such as gum and bone grafting or pocket reduction may be needed. If you need care from a periodontist or another dental professional, we refer you to carefully vetted colleagues.
- **Replacing lost teeth:** if a tooth has been lost to gum disease, [dental implants](/restorative-dentistry/dental-implants/) can provide a stable, natural-looking replacement.
