## What Is a Root Canal?

Inside every tooth is soft tissue called the dental pulp, which contains nerves, blood vessels and connective tissue. When the pulp becomes infected through deep decay, cracks, trauma or repeated dental procedures, it can cause significant pain and inflammation. A root canal removes the infected pulp and seals the tooth so bacteria can't get back in.

### Common causes of infection

- Deep tooth decay
- Cracked or chipped teeth
- Trauma or injury to the tooth
- Repeated dental procedures on the same tooth
- Untreated cavities that spread deeper into the tooth

## Signs You May Need a Root Canal

- Persistent or severe tooth pain
- Pain when chewing or biting
- Sensitivity to hot or cold that lingers
- Swelling or tenderness in the gums
- Darkening or discoloration of a tooth
- A pimple-like bump on the gums near the tooth

These symptoms don't always mean you need a root canal, but they should be checked. Severe pain or swelling? See [emergency dentistry](/general-dentistry/emergency-dentistry/).

---

## What Happens During Treatment

1. **Exam and X-rays:** the dentist evaluates the tooth and may take X-rays to see the extent of the infection.
2. **Numbing:** local anesthesia numbs the area so you stay comfortable.
3. **Removing the infected pulp:** a small opening is made in the tooth, and the diseased pulp is removed. The canals are cleaned and shaped.
4. **Disinfecting and sealing:** the canals are disinfected and filled with a biocompatible material, and a temporary or permanent filling closes the opening.
5. **Restoring the tooth:** for many patients, a [dental crown](/restorative-dentistry/dental-crowns/) is recommended to restore the tooth's strength, shape and chewing function.

---

## Does a Root Canal Hurt?

Local anesthesia numbs the area during treatment, and removing the infected pulp relieves the pressure and inflammation that cause severe tooth pain. Some mild tenderness may occur for a short time afterward.

---

## Recovery

Recovery is usually straightforward. Most patients return to normal daily activities shortly after treatment. Good oral hygiene and regular checkups help keep the treated tooth healthy.

---

## Root Canal vs. Tooth Extraction

Saving your natural tooth is usually the preferred approach when possible. Keeping the tooth helps maintain your bite and prevents neighboring teeth from shifting. In many cases, a root canal is also more cost-effective than [extracting the tooth](/general-dentistry/tooth-extraction/) and replacing it with a dental implant or bridge.

## Root Canal FAQs

### Does a root canal hurt?
Root canal treatment is performed under local anesthesia, so the area is numb. Removing the infected pulp relieves the pressure that causes severe tooth pain, and any tenderness afterward is usually mild and short-lived.

### What are the signs that I need a root canal?
Common signs include persistent or severe tooth pain, pain when biting, lingering sensitivity to hot or cold, swollen or tender gums, a darkening tooth, or a pimple-like bump on the gums near the tooth.

### Do I need a crown after a root canal?
For many patients, yes. A crown covers the treated tooth and helps restore its strength, shape and chewing function.

### Is it better to get a root canal or pull the tooth?
Saving the natural tooth is usually preferred when possible because it helps maintain your bite and prevents neighboring teeth from shifting. In many cases, a root canal is also more cost-effective than extraction followed by an implant or bridge.
