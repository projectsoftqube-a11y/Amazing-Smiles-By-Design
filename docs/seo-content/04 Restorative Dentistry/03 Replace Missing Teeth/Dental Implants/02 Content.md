## A Long-Lasting Solution for Missing Teeth

Missing teeth can affect more than your appearance. They can change the way you chew, affect your speech and gradually weaken the bone in your jaw. Dental implants replace the entire structure of a missing tooth, from the root to the visible crown, so they offer stability, comfort and a natural look that closely mimics real teeth.

---

## How Dental Implants Work

Over time, the implant bonds with the surrounding bone through a process called osseointegration. This creates a strong foundation. A connector piece called an abutment is then attached, and a custom crown is placed on top.

1. **Implant placement:** the implant is positioned in the jawbone during a minor surgical procedure.
2. **Healing and osseointegration:** over the next several months, the implant bonds with the bone.
3. **Abutment placement:** a small connector is attached to support the crown.
4. **Custom crown placement:** the final crown is secured, restoring the look and function of the tooth.

A single implant replaces one missing tooth without affecting the teeth beside it.

## Are Dental Implants Right for You?

Good candidates typically:

- Have one or more missing teeth
- Have healthy gums
- Have enough jawbone density to support an implant
- Maintain good overall oral health
- Don't smoke, or are willing to stop during healing

If bone density is too low, bone grafting may be recommended to strengthen the area first. Age alone usually isn't a limiting factor, but the jawbone must be fully developed. A consultation lets the dentist review your oral health, medical history and goals.

## Implant Planning With Advanced Imaging

Before treatment, detailed imaging, such as a CBCT scan, is used to assess:

- Bone density and volume
- The position of nearby nerves and sinus structures
- The alignment of surrounding teeth
- The best angle and depth for each implant

This planning helps position implants for stability and long-term success. [Our CBCT and imaging technology](/patient-information/advanced-technology/)

## What Happens During Implant Surgery

Implant placement is performed under local anesthesia. Ask us about dental sedation options.

1. A small opening is made in the gum tissue.
2. A specialized instrument creates space in the jawbone.
3. The implant post is placed into the bone.
4. The gum tissue is closed so it can heal.

Over the following months, the implant integrates with the bone before the final crown or restoration is placed.

---

## Recovery and Care

Full integration typically takes several months, but most patients return to normal daily activities shortly after surgery. During healing:

- Eat soft foods at first
- Keep up good oral hygiene
- Take any prescribed medication as directed
- Attend your follow-up appointments

With proper care, many implants remain functional for decades.
