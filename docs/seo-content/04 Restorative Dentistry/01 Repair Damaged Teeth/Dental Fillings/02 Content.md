## Why Fillings Are Needed

Tooth decay is one of the most common dental conditions in both children and adults. When bacteria break down enamel, they create cavities that can keep spreading deeper into the tooth if left untreated. A filling may be recommended for:

- Tooth decay or cavities
- Small cracks in the tooth
- Chipped teeth
- Areas weakened by minor fractures

Treating these problems early helps stop the damage from spreading and can help you avoid more complex treatment such as a [crown](/restorative-dentistry/dental-crowns/) or [root canal](/restorative-dentistry/non-surgical-root-canal/) later.

## Signs You May Need a Filling

Some cavities develop slowly without obvious symptoms and are found at a routine exam or on digital X-rays. Other times you may notice:

- Sensitivity to hot, cold or sweet foods
- Pain when biting or chewing
- A rough or chipped area on the tooth
- Visible dark spots or small holes on the tooth

---

## What to Expect

Placing a filling is a routine, typically quick and comfortable treatment:

1. The tooth is isolated to keep it clean and dry.
2. Decayed or damaged tooth structure is removed.
3. A conditioning solution and bonding agent are applied.
4. Composite resin is placed in layers and shaped to rebuild the tooth.
5. Each layer is hardened with a curing light.
6. The filling is adjusted, smoothed and polished to fit your bite.

Afterward, the tooth looks and works like a normal tooth again.

---

## Tooth-Colored vs. Metal Fillings

Composite resin, often called tooth-colored or white fillings, is made from acrylic resin and finely ground glass particles. It bonds directly to the tooth, so the dentist can often remove less healthy tooth structure. Benefits include:

- A natural, tooth-colored appearance
- Strong bonding to the tooth
- More natural tooth preserved
- A durable, long-lasting, metal-free restoration

Older amalgam fillings are made from a mix of metals such as mercury, silver, tin and copper. According to the American Dental Association, both amalgam and composite are considered safe and effective. Many patients choose composite for its natural look and metal-free composition.

## Filling, Inlay or Onlay?

Fillings are used when the damaged area is relatively small. When a tooth has more extensive damage, an [inlay or onlay](/restorative-dentistry/inlays-onlays/), a custom restoration usually made from porcelain in a dental laboratory, may be recommended instead.

## Preventing Cavities

- Visit the dentist regularly for cleanings and exams
- Brush after meals with fluoride toothpaste
- Floss daily
- Drink water instead of sugary or acidic drinks
- Use an antimicrobial mouthwash when recommended
- Avoid tobacco products

## Dental Filling FAQs

### Does getting a filling hurt?
Placing a filling is a routine treatment that is typically quick and comfortable. The dentist removes the decay, then rebuilds the tooth with composite resin hardened by a curing light.

### What are tooth-colored fillings made of?
Tooth-colored (composite resin) fillings are made from acrylic resin and finely ground glass particles. They bond directly to the tooth and can be color-matched to your surrounding teeth.

### Are composite fillings safe?
According to the American Dental Association, both amalgam and composite fillings are considered safe and effective for restoring teeth.

### How do I know if I need a filling?
Signs include sensitivity to hot, cold or sweet foods, pain when biting, a rough or chipped area, or dark spots on a tooth. Many cavities have no symptoms and are found at a routine exam or on digital X-rays.
