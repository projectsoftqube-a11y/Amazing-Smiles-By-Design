## When You Need a Crown

Teeth can weaken over time because of decay, injury, large fillings or general wear. The dentist may recommend a crown to:

- Protect a weak tooth from breaking
- Restore a cracked, chipped or fractured tooth
- Cover a tooth after root canal treatment
- Support a tooth with a very large filling
- Restore a dental implant
- Anchor a dental bridge that replaces missing teeth
- Strengthen teeth weakened by decay or wear
- Improve the look of discolored or misshapen teeth

In many cases, a crown can save a tooth that might otherwise need to be extracted.

---

## How Crowns Protect Teeth

A damaged tooth is more vulnerable to bacteria, decay and further breakage. Because a crown covers the whole tooth, it acts as a barrier against acids produced by bacteria, sugars and starches, food particles that enter cracks, and additional fractures.

---

## Crowns That Improve Your Smile

Crowns can also correct cosmetic concerns such as severely discolored teeth that don't respond to whitening, uneven or misshapen teeth, chips, small gaps and worn-down teeth. Modern crown materials closely mimic the natural translucency and color of enamel.

---

## Types of Dental Crowns

- **Porcelain crowns:** a natural appearance that matches the color and translucency of your teeth; ideal for visible areas.
- **Porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM) crowns:** a metal base for strength with a natural-looking porcelain exterior.
- **Zirconia crowns:** metal-free, very durable and fracture resistant.
- **Metal crowns:** gold or other alloys; extremely strong and typically used on back teeth.

The dentist will recommend the best material for the tooth's condition and location and your goals.

---

## The Dental Crown Process

Placing a crown typically takes two visits.

### First visit: preparing the tooth
Any decay or damage is removed and the tooth is reshaped so the crown fits securely. An impression or digital scan is taken, and a temporary crown protects the tooth while your permanent crown is made in a dental laboratory.

### Second visit: placing the final crown
The temporary crown is removed. The dentist checks your permanent crown for fit, comfort and appearance, then bonds it in place. Afterward, the crown works like a natural tooth.

---

## Crowns vs. Fillings and Onlays

A [filling](/restorative-dentistry/dental-fillings/) repairs a small cavity. An [onlay](/restorative-dentistry/inlays-onlays/) rebuilds part of the chewing surface and preserves more natural tooth. A crown covers the entire tooth and is used when the tooth needs the most protection, such as after a [root canal](/restorative-dentistry/non-surgical-root-canal/).

## Dental Crown FAQs

### How long do dental crowns last?
Many dental crowns remain functional for 15 to 20 years or longer with good brushing and flossing, regular dental visits and avoiding grinding or chewing on hard objects.

### How many visits does a crown take?
Placing a dental crown typically takes two visits: one to prepare the tooth and place a temporary crown, and one to bond the permanent crown.

### Will my crown look natural?
Crowns are custom-designed to match the color, shape and size of your natural teeth. In many cases, patients cannot tell a crown from their natural teeth.

### Do I need a crown after a root canal?
For many patients, yes. A crown covers the treated tooth and helps restore its strength, shape and normal chewing function.
